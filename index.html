Rotation 541–5424 Q
Q1

Right ulnar nerve laceration, repaired 7 days ago. Most appropriate rehabilitation management?

  1. TENS
  2. ROM exercise
  3. Shockwave
  4. One option missing
Reveal answer
Answer: Protected ROM only. If forced to choose from the visible options: ROM exercise.
Fresh nerve repair must not be stretched aggressively; protective splinting may be preferable if that was the missing choice.
Q2

Which finding occurs during the flaccid phase after stroke?

  1. Claw toe
  2. Adhesive capsulitis
  3. Increased space between acromion and humeral head
  4. Tight calf
Reveal answer
Answer: Increased space between the acromion and humeral head → inferior shoulder subluxation.
Q3

Common pressure-injury site in side-lying?

  1. Breast
  2. Heel
  3. Iliac crest
  4. One option missing
Reveal answer
Answer: Greater trochanter if offered; among the visible choices, iliac crest.
Q4

Which exercise improves flexibility?

  1. Marching
  2. “Indoor way”
  3. Tandem walking
  4. Stepping
Reveal answer
Answer: Cannot be determined reliably from the recalled choices.
Flexibility is primarily trained by stretching; the stem or one option was likely recalled incorrectly.
Rotation 539–5408 Q
Q1

What does FABER test assess?

  1. Hip joint pathology
  2. Radicular pain
Reveal answer
Answer: Hip / sacroiliac region pathology; if choosing between the recalled options: hip joint pathology.
Q2

How do you calculate %HRmax and %heart-rate reserve?

Reveal answer
Answer: HRmax ≈ 220 − age. %HRmax = exercise HR / HRmax × 100. HRR = HRmax − resting HR. %HRR = (exercise HR − resting HR) / HRR × 100.
Q3

Older patient with foot drop. Appropriate orthosis?

Reveal answer
Answer: Ankle-foot orthosis (AFO).
Q4

Stroke patient circumducts the affected leg and drags the foot. Management?

Reveal answer
Answer: AFO, if impaired dorsiflexion / foot drop contributes to toe drag.
Circumduction can have other causes, so AFO is not automatic in every case.
Q5

Older adult walks with the trunk leaning sideways. What compensatory gait is present?

Reveal answer
Answer: Likely compensated Trendelenburg gait, if the trunk leans toward the stance limb.

Rotation 541–54215 Q
Q1

Give 2 consequences of persistent residual-limb edema after amputation.

Reveal answer
  • Delayed residual-limb shaping → difficult prosthetic fitting.
  • Skin / wound problems and discomfort.
Q2

Exercise during the thawing phase of frozen shoulder.

Reveal answer
  • Progressive active-assisted and active ROM.
  • Gentle sustained stretching.
  • Progress shoulder / scapular strengthening as ROM improves.
Exam pearl: Thawing = ROM improving → restore ROM + gradually strengthen.
Q3

Timing and goals of acute-phase versus rehabilitation-phase stroke care.

Reveal answer
  • Acute phase: early assessment, positioning, swallowing safety, ROM and complication prevention.
  • Mobilize when medically stable; avoid aggressive high-dose mobilization in the first 24 h.
  • Rehabilitation phase: active multidisciplinary functional training once stable and able to participate.
  • Goal: acute = prevent complications; rehab = restore / maximize function, ADL and participation.
Exam pearl: Acute = prevent complication. Rehab = restore function.
Q4

Criterion for entering an ageing society.

Reveal answer
  • Thai convention: age ≥60 years >10%.
  • Alternative convention: age ≥65 years >7%.
  • For ≥60 years: 10% ageing → 20% complete-aged → 28% super-aged.
Exam pearl: Memorize the Thai ≥60-year thresholds first.
Q5

Ageing characteristics relevant to rehabilitation.

Reveal answer
  • Reduced physiological reserve and exercise tolerance.
  • Sarcopenia / reduced strength, flexibility and bone strength.
  • Reduced visual, vestibular and proprioceptive function → impaired balance.
  • Multimorbidity, polypharmacy and slower recovery.
  • Cognitive, psychological, nutritional and social issues may limit participation.
Exam pearl: Backup mnemonic: RAMPS = Reduced reserve, Atypical presentation, Multiple pathology, Polypharmacy, Social adversity.
Q6

Principle of balance testing in older adults.

Reveal answer
  • Assess control of center of mass relative to base of support.
  • Assess both static and dynamic balance.
  • Progress easier → harder: feet together → semi-tandem → tandem → single-leg stance.
  • Use standardized tests and guard against falls.
Exam pearl: Testing ≠ training.
Q7

Identify axillary crutches and give one indication.

Reveal answer
  • Axillary crutches.
  • Example: unilateral lower-limb fracture requiring non-weight-bearing, with adequate upper-limb strength and balance.
  • Weight is supported through the hands, not the axillae.
Q8

Identify a single-point cane and explain measurement.

Reveal answer
  • Handle at wrist crease / greater trochanter level while standing in usual shoes.
  • About 20–30° elbow flexion when holding.
  • Usually hold it opposite the weak or painful leg.
Q9

Which muscles should be exercised after lower-limb amputation?

Reveal answer
  • Hip extensors and abductors are especially important.
  • If the knee is preserved: quadriceps and hamstrings.
  • Also strengthen the intact leg, trunk and upper limbs for transfers / gait aids.
Exam pearl: Exact program depends on amputation level.
Q10

Give 2 therapeutic-exercise components for mild knee OA.

Reveal answer
  • Quadriceps strengthening.
  • ROM / flexibility exercise for the knee and tight surrounding muscles.
Exam pearl: Low-impact aerobic exercise is also useful.
Q11

Stroke discharge planning: grade-4 power, tripod cane, poorly controlled HT, smoker, sleeps on floor. Give 4 points.

Reveal answer
  • Secondary prevention: BP control, adherence, smoking cessation and follow-up.
  • Home modification: suitable-height bed, safe bathroom, clear walking route.
  • Functional safety: safe transfers, ADL and correct cane use.
  • Continuity: home exercise, caregiver education and rehab follow-up.
Q12

Advice for phantom sensation.

Reveal answer
  • Explain that feeling the missing limb is common after amputation.
  • If painless, specific analgesic treatment is usually unnecessary.
  • Distinguish phantom sensation from phantom limb pain.
  • Warn the patient not to try to stand on the perceived missing limb.
Q13

Back pain worsened by coughing / sneezing / straining. Flexion or extension exercise?

Reveal answer
  • Likely exam answer: extension-biased exercise for disc-related pain / HNP.
  • Clinically, continue a direction only if symptoms improve or centralize; stop if symptoms peripheralize.
Exam pearl: Exam key often says disc → extension, but real practice uses directional preference.
Q14

Back / leg symptoms improve by sitting; SLR and Kemp negative. Give 2 differential diagnoses.

Reveal answer
  • Lumbar spinal stenosis with neurogenic claudication.
  • Vascular claudication / peripheral arterial disease.
Exam pearl: If the original stem was mainly axial back pain, lumbar spondylosis may have been the intended second answer.
Q15

How should airway clearance be performed in COPD?

Reveal answer
  • ACBT: breathing control → thoracic expansion → huffing.
  • Effective cough when secretions move proximally.
  • Mobilization; add PEP / OPEP or positioning when appropriate.
Exam pearl: Dyspnea → pursed-lip breathing. Sputum → ACBT + huff + cough.

Q1

Principle of balance training in older adults.

Reveal answer
  • Progressively challenge balance while maintaining safety.
  • Reduce base of support and hand support as ability improves.
  • Progress static → dynamic tasks.
  • Train weight shifting, reaching, stepping, turning and walking.
  • Combine with lower-limb strengthening.
Exam pearl: Progressive challenge without causing falls.

Q2

Football player after leg amputation cries and isolates himself. Management?

Reveal answer
  • Establish rapport and acknowledge the loss.
  • Assess mood, sleep, appetite, rehab participation and suicidal thoughts.
  • Differentiate normal grief / adjustment from major depression.
  • Involve rehabilitation psychology / psychiatry when indicated.
  • Use family / peer support and realistic functional / prosthetic goals.
Exam pearl: Exam priority: psychological assessment + support + depression / suicide screen.
Rotation 533–5341 Q
Q1

After leg amputation, patient feels shooting / electric pain “at the amputated area.” Management?

Reveal answer
  • First determine whether pain is in the missing limb or the residual limb.
  • Missing limb → phantom limb pain.
  • Residual limb → inspect for wound, infection, ischemia, pressure / socket problem or neuroma.
  • Use individualized multimodal pain management; mirror therapy can be considered for phantom pain.
Exam pearl: Do not automatically label every electric pain after amputation as phantom pain.
Rotation 531–5322 Q
Q1

Identify tripod cane and give one indication.

Reveal answer
  • Tripod cane.
  • Example: mild post-stroke hemiparesis needing extra balance support.
  • Not enough for strict non-weight-bearing.
Q2

Principles of flexibility exercise in older adults.

Reveal answer
  • Warm up first.
  • Stretch slowly to mild tension, not pain.
  • Avoid bouncing and breath-holding.
  • Hold static stretches about 30–60 s as tolerated.
  • Repeat regularly, typically ≥2–3 days/week.
  • Modify for osteoporosis, joint disease and balance risk.
Rotation 527–5285 Q
Q1

Complications and prevention in a bedridden patient.

Reveal answer
  • Pressure injury → repositioning, pressure redistribution, skin inspection, heel off-loading.
  • Contracture → proper positioning + regular ROM.
  • Muscle atrophy / deconditioning → early mobilization + progressive exercise.
  • DVT / VTE → risk assessment, prophylaxis and mobilization.
  • Atelectasis / secretion retention → upright positioning, mobilization, breathing / cough techniques.
  • Constipation → fluid, fiber, mobility and bowel regimen.
  • Orthostatic intolerance → gradual sitting → standing progression.
Exam pearl: Original clinical details were missing, so these are general scoring points.

Q5

Exercise and positioning to prevent contracture after below-knee amputation.

Reveal answer
  • Maintain full knee extension and hip extension.
  • Quadriceps strengthening and knee-extension ROM.
  • Hip-extension exercise; prone lying when appropriate.
  • Avoid prolonged sitting with the knee flexed.
  • Do not place a pillow under the knee.
Exam pearl: Main BKA contractures: hip flexion + knee flexion.

COPD

  • Residual-limb pain = pain in the stump → look for local cause.
