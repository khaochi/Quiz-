Stroke & Neurorehabilitation8 Q
S01

Acute stroke: give 3 complications and one prevention strategy for each.

Reveal answer
  • Pressure injury → reposition regularly, pressure relief, skin care.
  • Contracture → correct positioning + daily passive/active ROM.
  • DVT → early mobilization when safe + ankle pumps ± prophylaxis as indicated.
  • Aspiration/pneumonia → swallow screening, safe feeding position/texture.
  • Shoulder subluxation → support the arm; avoid traction.
S02

MCA infarction with hemispatial neglect, motor grade 2 and poor sitting control: list 3 problems and a goal for each.

Reveal answer
  • Hemiparesis → improve motor control/strength and functional use.
  • Neglect → improve awareness/scanning toward the affected side.
  • Poor trunk/sitting balance → achieve stable independent sitting and midline orientation.
S03

Name the 3 phases of stroke rehabilitation and give the main goal of each.

Reveal answer
  • Acute phase → medical stabilization, swallow/positioning, prevent secondary complications, early safe mobility.
  • Subacute / intensive rehabilitation → maximize motor recovery, ADL, communication and mobility.
  • Chronic / community phase → maintain gains, maximize independence/participation, prevent recurrence and deconditioning.
S04

Criteria for admission to intensive inpatient stroke rehabilitation.

Reveal answer
  • Medically and neurologically stable.
  • Meaningful functional deficits requiring multidisciplinary therapy.
  • Sufficient alertness/cognition to participate.
  • Adequate cardiopulmonary endurance/tolerance.
  • Realistic rehabilitation goals and recovery potential.
  • No uncontrolled infection or hemodynamic instability.
S05

Stroke discharge planning: patient walks with tripod cane/minimal assist, smokes, BP control is poor, and the home has stairs / room-location barriers.

Reveal answer
  • Confirm safe mobility/ADL and correct gait aid.
  • Assess and modify home barriers: stairs, rails, bathroom, bedroom location.
  • Arrange caregiver/community/home-health support.
  • Continue PT/OT ± speech/swallow follow-up.
  • Secondary prevention: BP control, smoking cessation, medication adherence.
  • Educate patient/caregiver on falls, transfers and stroke warning signs.
S06

Stroke prognosis: prior athlete, severe CAD, strong family support and motivation, motor grade 1, contralateral OA knee. List positive and negative factors.

Reveal answer
  • Positive: previously fit / athletic.
  • Positive: motivated.
  • Positive: strong family support.
  • Negative: severe motor deficit.
  • Negative: severe CAD limits endurance.
  • Negative: OA knee may limit mobility/training.
S07

Swallow screening: outline a concise 3-step approach.

Reveal answer
  • 1) Check alertness, upright sitting, oral motor control/secretions and voluntary cough.
  • 2) Trial a small amount of water while observing cough/choking, wet voice and respiratory distress.
  • 3) If safe, progress volume/texture; if coughing/choking/wet voice occurs, stop oral intake and refer for formal assessment.
S08

Spastic hemiplegic limbs: give 4 specific complications and prevention.

Reveal answer
  • Shoulder adduction/internal-rotation contracture → positioning + ROM.
  • Elbow/wrist/finger flexion contracture → stretching/ROM ± splinting.

Pulmonary Rehabilitation8 Q
S14

Give positions to reduce dyspnea and the principle behind them.

Reveal answer
  • Sit leaning forward with forearms supported on thighs/table, or stand leaning forward with arms supported.
  • Fixing the shoulder girdle lets accessory inspiratory muscles assist rib-cage expansion.
  • This reduces work of breathing / dyspnea.
S15

Differentiate cough and huff for airway clearance.

Reveal answer
  • Cough: glottis closes → high intrathoracic pressure → explosive expiration.
  • Huff/FET: glottis stays open → forced expiration saying 'huff'.
  • Huff generally causes less airway collapse/fatigue and moves secretions centrally.
  • Cough is useful to expel central secretions.
S16

Give 4 contraindications / precautions for chest percussion.

Reveal answer
  • Rib fracture or severe osteoporosis.
  • Bone metastasis at treatment site.
  • Active bleeding/hemoptysis or major coagulopathy.
  • Severe pain or recent thoracic surgery/wound.
  • Unstable cardiovascular status.
  • Avoid direct percussion over spine, sternum, kidneys and bony prominences.
S17

Give 4 precautions / contraindications for postural drainage.

Reveal answer
  • Unstable hemodynamic/cardiovascular status.
  • Raised ICP or recent neurosurgery when head-down is unsafe.
  • Severe reflux/aspiration risk or immediately after feeding.
  • Significant hemoptysis.
  • Acute spinal instability.
  • Severe dyspnea/hypoxemia unable to tolerate the position.
S18

COPD: give the main exercise components of pulmonary rehabilitation.

Reveal answer
  • Aerobic/endurance exercise.
  • Resistance training for upper and lower limbs.
  • Individualize intensity to symptoms and exercise capacity.
  • Add flexibility and inspiratory-muscle training when indicated.
S19

COPD with pneumonia on a ventilator: what airway-clearance method and why?

Reveal answer
  • Positioning plus endotracheal suctioning, with secretion-mobilization techniques as indicated.
  • Reason: an intubated patient cannot independently generate an effective cough.
S20

COPD/pneumonia ready for discharge but still has sputum: teach airway clearance.

Reveal answer
  • Active-cycle breathing / huff + effective cough.
  • Hydration and mobility.
  • Patient is alert and can clear secretions independently.
S21

Describe pursed-lip breathing.

Reveal answer
  • Inhale slowly through the nose.
  • Exhale gently through nearly closed lips.
  • Expiration should be about twice as long as inspiration.
  • Do not forcefully blow.
