Q1

Which finding is an impairment rather than an activity limitation/disability?

  1. Communication problem
  2. Eating difficulty
  3. Homonymous hemianopia
  4. Cannot walk
Reveal answer
Best answer: Homonymous hemianopia.
ICF: loss/abnormality of body function or structure = impairment; difficulty performing an activity = activity limitation.

Q6
Seen in: 513–514, 505–506

Post-stroke patient has BP about 180–190/110 mmHg despite neurologic stability. What should be done regarding active/intensive exercise?

  1. No active exercise
  2. Early mobilization
  3. Early mobilization + swallowing screening
  4. Intensive rehabilitation
Reveal answer
Best answer: Defer intensive/aerobic rehabilitation and obtain medical review/BP management before progressing.
A blanket “no active exercise” rule is too absolute. At about 180–190/110 mmHg, intensive exercise is not appropriate without review, but low-intensity mobilization may still be considered depending on stroke type/timing, reperfusion status, symptoms and the local protocol.

What does gabapentin mainly help in spinal stenosis?

  2. Improve numbness directly

Reveal answer
Exam-intended concept: neuropathic/radicular pain rather than axial mechanical pain or direct reversal of numbness.
Clinical update: do not memorize this as a recommendation to routinely prescribe gabapentin for lumbar radicular pain/sciatica. Known cancer metastatic to the spine: which physical modalities should be avoided over the lesion?

  2. Therapeutic ultrasound
  3. Shockwave
  4. Lumbar traction
Reveal answer

  • AKA/transfemoral: prevent hip flexion, abduction and external-rotation contracture; maintain extension, adduction and neutral rotation.
  • Strengthen hip extensors/abductors, knee extensors when present, core and the intact limb for prosthetic gait.
Last-minute review only. These are the repeatedly tested distinctions and calculations—not a duplicate of the full bank.
Stroke
  • Impairment = body-function loss (e.g. hemianopia); activity limitation = cannot walk/eat/communicate.
  • Acute phase → prevent complications; rehabilitation phase → restore function; community phase → reintegration + secondary prevention.
  • Failed swallow screen → NPO first + formal assessment; NG is for ongoing enteral nutrition when indicated.
  • Flaccid shoulder → support arm and avoid traction; chronic toe drag from dorsiflexion weakness → consider AFO.
Spine
  • Disc pattern: flexion/cough may worsen radicular pain; trial extension only when it centralizes symptoms.
  • Stenosis: standing/extension/downhill worse; sitting/flexion/shopping-cart posture better.
  • C7 = middle finger + triceps; C6 = thumb/lateral hand + biceps/BR; L4 ≈ ankle dorsiflexion/medial leg; L5 = great toe/dorsum foot.
  • Saddle numbness + bladder/bowel change = urgent cauda-equina red flag.
Shoulder / Hand
  • CTS → nocturnal paresthesia relieved by shaking; neutral wrist splint; APB/thenar weakness if advanced.
  • De Quervain → Finkelstein + thumb spica.
  • Tennis elbow → forceful grip/resisted wrist extension; Cozen positive; counterforce strap is common.
  • Frozen shoulder painful phase = pain control + gentle ROM/isometric; thawing = progressive ROM/stretching + strengthening.
Knee / Foot
  • Knee OA → quadriceps setting during painful phase, then progressive resistance + ROM/flexibility.
  • Plantar fasciopathy → plantar-fascia + calf stretch; limited ankle dorsiflexion is a risk factor; flat foot → intrinsic-foot strengthening/support.
Gait aids
  • Cane on the opposite side of the painful/weak leg; grip at wrist crease with 20–30° elbow flexion.
  • Strict NWB → 2 crutches/walker, not a single cane. Forearm crutches suit longer-term community mobility.
  • Three-point gait is the classic pattern when one lower limb is non-weight-bearing with two crutches.
Pulmonary
  • COPD dyspnea → pursed-lip breathing; productive secretions → ACBT/huff/cough.
  • Atelectasis → deep-inspiration/expansion exercise.
  • COPD diagnosis: post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC < 0.70 confirms persistent airflow obstruction in the GOLD framework; FEV1 grades airflow-limitation severity.
Amputation
  • BKA: prevent hip/knee flexion contracture; AKA: prevent hip flexion/abduction/external-rotation contracture and maintain neutral alignment.
  • Persistent edema delays prosthetic fitting and harms skin/wound; focal tender stump nodule = neuroma.
  • Phantom sensation is non-painful and common; phantom pain is painful neuropathic perception.
Exam caveats / ambiguous recalls
  • Q21 NWB: among the listed options, choose C. Lofstrand/forearm crutches; strict NWB needs bilateral support.
  • Q24 cerebellar ataxia: “walker” is not listed. With marked/unsafe gait instability, B. wheelchair is the defensible listed answer; a walker may be used in practice after assessment.
  • Q60 spinal metastasis: the recall has multiple defensible “avoid” choices—ultrasound, shockwave/deep heat and traction over malignant/unstable bone.
  • Q68 lateral epicondylitis: forceful grip and repetitive wrist extension both aggravate symptoms; if forced to one classic movement choice, use D. repetitive wrist extension.
  • Q79 knee OA: current guidance favors NSAID-based treatment when appropriate, especially topical NSAID; an older/local exam key may still say acetaminophen for “mild” disease.
  • Q86 acute heel pain: oral NSAID and cold symptom relief are both defensible; the exact intended answer depends on the original exam wording/key.
  • Q13, Q23, Q74, Q100, Q103: recalled stems are incomplete or flawed; no single reliable key should be memorized.
  • Evidence-sensitive updates: Q50 gabapentin, Q78 knee-brace rationale, Q87 COPD spirometry, and SEQ Q13 TENS are worded to separate an exam concept from current clinical guidance.
Numbers
  • HRmax ≈ 220 − age. Karvonen: target HR = resting HR + intensity × (HRmax − resting HR).
  • Age 40, rest 60, 85% HRR = 162 bpm. Age 70, 75% HRmax = 112.5 bpm.
